### 1. Executive Summary 

This proposal presents a digital application for the Faculty of Management Studies and Commerce to manage complaints, problems, and improvement suggestions submitted by students and lecturers. The proposed system will provide a structured channel for reporting issues with image or video evidence, classifying each submission as a complaint or suggestion, marking priority as urgent or normal, and assigning responsibility to the relevant authority for action. 

The proposed app will support the full case lifecycle from submission to closure. After a case is submitted, an administrator will review it, assign it to the appropriate authority, monitor progress, and ensure that the informed user receives a message when the case is solved. The system will also provide dashboards showing attempted cases, not attempted cases, open cases, not-started cases, ageing reports, authority-wise progress, and priority-based summaries. 

The app is recommended as a practical digital governance tool that improves transparency, accountability, responsiveness, and evidence-based decision-making within the faculty. 

### 2. Background and Problem Statement 

In a busy faculty environment, students and lecturers frequently observe issues related to classrooms, IT facilities, administration, safety, cleanliness, examination services, academic processes, infrastructure, and student support services. These issues are often communicated informally through verbal messages, phone calls, WhatsApp messages, emails, or personal visits to offices. Such methods are useful for immediate communication, but they do not provide a systematic mechanism for recording, assigning, tracking, and reporting actions taken. 

The absence of a centralized system may result in duplicated complaints, delayed responses, unclear responsibility, loss of evidence, and difficulty in identifying repeated problems. It may also be difficult for faculty management to know how many issues are pending, which authority is responsible, how long a case has remained not started, and whether urgent matters are receiving timely attention. 

Therefore, the faculty requires a simple, secure, mobile-friendly app that allows reporting users to submit complaints and suggestions and allows administrators to manage these submissions through a formal workflow.

3. Objectives of the Proposed App 

- Provide students and lecturers with a convenient method to report complaints, problems, and improvement suggestions. 

- Allow users to attach images and videos as supporting evidence. 

- Enable classification of submissions as complaints or suggestions and marking of priority as urgent or normal. 

- Provide administrators with tools to review cases and assign them to relevant authorities. 

- Maintain an editable authority list that can be updated when responsibilities change. 

- Notify the informed user when a case is solved. 

- Provide dashboards and ageing reports for monitoring open, attempted, not attempted, and not-started cases. 

- Support evidence-based decision-making and continuous improvement within the faculty. 

### 4. Proposed Solution 

The proposed solution is a web-based and mobile-friendly application named the FMSC Complaint and Suggestion Management App. The app will allow students and lecturers to submit complaints or suggestions using a simple form. Each case will include a title, description, case type, priority level, category, location, and optional image or video evidence. 

After submission, the case will be available to authorized administrators. The administrator will review the case, confirm or adjust category and priority, and assign it to a relevant authority. The assigned authority will update the progress of the case, add comments, upload evidence of completion if needed, and mark the case as solved. The reporting user will then receive a notification that the case has been solved. 

The system will provide a dashboard for administrators and management to monitor the number of cases, pending cases, urgent cases, not-started cases, ageing of not-started cases, and authority-wise progress.

### 8. Proposed Case Lifecycle 

The proposed system should follow a clear case lifecycle. This will help all users understand the present status of a complaint or suggestion. 

1. Submitted: The user submits a complaint or suggestion. 

2. Under Review: The administrator reads and verifies the case. 

3. Not Started: The case is accepted but action has not yet started. 

4. Assigned: The case is assigned to a relevant authority. 

5. In Progress: The authority starts action and updates progress. 

6. Waiting for Information: Further details are required from the user or another party. 

7. Solved: The authority or administrator records that the issue has been solved. 

8. Closed: The administrator closes the case after confirming completion. 

9. Rejected: The administrator rejects invalid, duplicate, inappropriate, or out-of-scope submissions. 

10. Reopened: The case is reopened if the resolution is not satisfactory.

### 18. Conclusion and Recommendation 

The proposed FMSC Complaint and Suggestion Management App is a practical and necessary digital solution for improving communication, accountability, responsiveness, and service quality within the Faculty of Management Studies and Commerce. It provides a structured method for students and lecturers to report issues and suggestions, while giving administrators and management a clear mechanism to assign responsibility, monitor progress, and identify recurring problems. 

It is recommended that the faculty proceed with a phased implementation, beginning with the minimum viable product covering case submission, evidence upload, admin review, authority assignment, progress tracking, solved notification, and the dashboard with ageing reports. Future enhancements can be introduced after pilot testing and feedback from users.
